House rules to agree before you start

11 house rules fired for this map, in the order they matter. Each one has a predicate over the numbers above; rules whose precondition is false are not printed at all. The last one always fires, because the rulebook demands that conversation and explicitly refuses to automate it.

  1. Play on Weekday. Service on the quieter weekend day drops to 16.2% of a weekday's trips, and every question that depends on being able to move gets worse with it.
    everyone must agree why?
  2. Start every round at CENTRAL PARK STAND 2. It touches 59.1% of the network's routes, so it is the only place from which the whole map is reachable inside the hiding period — and it is where the seekers will start anyway.
    why?
  3. Use exactly these borders: south 43.950205, west -92.541035, north 44.097356, east -92.406547. The rulebook is emphatic that every player must be using the same set of borders, and on this map the border decides which questions work at all. Copy the GeoJSON rather than redrawing it.
    everyone must agree why?
  4. Read the dead list out before the first round. 28 questions here return null or a known answer — 1st Administrative Division, 2nd Administrative Division, 3rd Administrative Division, Amusement Park, Aquarium… — and asking one costs the seekers a slot and pays the hider a card.
    everyone must agree why?
  5. Decide as a group whether anyone has to spend money during the game. Egg Partner, Impressionable Consumer and Lemon Phylactery all require a purchase; the rulebook flags the first two and is silent about the third, which is an inconsistency. Treat them as one switch.
    why?
  6. Set an end-of-game timer at 18:33. That is 30 minutes before the median last departure, which is the point after which a hider in an average zone can no longer get anywhere — including home.
    why?
  7. A SMALL game gives 10 minutes to answer a photo question and 5 minutes for everything else, and the Move powerup grants 10 minutes. Put a visible timer on it.
    why?
  8. Hand limit is 6, raised to 7 or 8 only by Draw 1 Expand 1. Going over forces an immediate play-or-discard, and time bonuses only count if you are still holding them at the end.
    why?
  9. Warn the hider that only 24.8% of the map is reachable inside the hiding period from the start location. Plan the journey before the clock starts — the rulebook's advice is to go somewhere you know you can get to.
    why?
  10. There is no rail mode in this feed, so High-Speed Train Line and Rail Station are dead. Brief the seekers: that is 2 questions that pay the hider a card and teach nothing.
    why?
  11. Before anything else, agree which areas are off the map because someone does not feel safe going there. The rulebook requires this conversation and explicitly refuses to automate it; this generator will not draw that polygon for you. Apply the outcome with --exclude-stop so every number on these pages matches the map you are actually playing.

What this data does not know

Honest limits of the sources, carried straight through from the layers that produced them.

  • 71.3% of restaurants carry a `cuisine` tag (92 of 129), so the 32 restaurants qualifying for Curse of the Distant Cuisine are a floor, not a total.
  • Candidate hiding spots are a shortlist for a human, never a verdict: OpenStreetMap does not know whether a plaza is locked at night, so the rulebook's “publicly accessible during all game hours” test cannot be automated.
  • Counts reflect one OpenStreetMap snapshot; the mirror that answered is not recorded, because recording it would make two identical runs produce different pages. Cached responses keep reruns byte-identical.
  • Cuisine tokens rejected as dishes or super-national regions rather than countries: arab, asian, bar_and_grill, barbecue, breakfast, burger, dessert, fine_dining, ice_cream, international, kebab, mediterranean…. Adjective tokens only — promoting a dish to a country would change the count.
  • Distances are measured to a computed area centroid, not to a map app's label anchor. On this map's park polygons the two differ by up to 71 m at the 90th percentile, which is a real fraction of the 402 m zone radius.
  • Every OpenStreetMap count here is a lower bound on what the seekers' map app will show. OSM is strong on parks, schools, places of worship, hospitals and libraries and materially incomplete on retail, restaurants and chains.
  • No named peak or volcano is mapped inside the border. A map app may still label a hill from its own gazetteer, so treat the mountain questions as dead-with-a-caveat.
  • OSM has no review count, so the rulebook's “5 or more Google Reviews” legitimacy test is approximated by requiring a `name` tag. Measured effect on this kind of feed: a 5–10% trim, in the right direction, but not the same function.
  • OpenStreetMap tags `natural=coastline` on ocean and sea shorelines only, so a great lake or inland sea carries none. A zero here means “no ocean coast in the border”, not “no large water”.
  • The “within 10 ft of a routable path” test could not be evaluated (the Overpass proximity join failed), so every candidate spot below is marked verify-on-the-ground.
  • These queries returned a floor rather than a total and are marked partial: legal-spot-path-filter.
