How Zones Are Scored

Four axes, one holistic verdict

Choosing the right hiding zone is the single most impactful decision of your run — and the rulebook offers no universally "right" answer. Every zone must be centered on a transit station; in a MEDIUM game like this one that means a ¼-mile circle around the stop's map icon, and you must stay inside it for your entire round. You get exactly the 60-minute hiding period, on the pre-established transit system only, to reach it: if the timer ends while you're somewhere else, that's your zone now.

Each of the ten scouted zones below was geocoded, its circle inventoried from OpenStreetMap, and scored 0–10 on the four axes here. The overall score is a holistic judgment, not an average — a zone with a fatal flaw (no Sunday service, one obvious hiding spot) ranks below its arithmetic.

A1Transit access

Can you reliably reach the zone from Rapid Central inside the 60-minute hiding period — on this day of the week — and can you leave it again? Frequent onward service keeps the Move powerup alive (it can't be played in the end game, so a stranded hider has no escape), while hourly tails are both hard to reach and easy for seekers to enumerate. Verified two-seat transfers on this network routinely bust the window.

A2Hideability

The edge-vs-density trade-off. Sparse edge zones are harder to identify and travel to, but once seekers narrow the map, the lack of nearby stations gives you away; dense areas offer the protection of complexity but hand seekers fast transit and expose you to a devastating tentacle. Photo questions decide this axis too: a station view of generic low-rise sprawl leaks almost nothing, a signature skyline leaks everything — and since Street View is banned for research, a visually anonymous zone stays anonymous.

A3Endgame spots

Once seekers enter your zone off-transit, you lock in place: the spot must be publicly accessible during all game hours, within 10 feet of a marked path or road, and somewhere a long stay raises no suspicion — which is why the rulebook says avoid stores and businesses outright. Zones rich in path-laced parks, plazas and civic buildings score high; a clever spot can buy an hour even after your station is found.

A4Amenities

You may be in this circle for many hours. Is there a bathroom you can actually access? Food and water? Shade or shelter? Small stops may have none of these — the rulebook tells hiders to identify restaurants or stores in the zone before committing. Comfort is strategy: photo questions arrive on a 10-minute timer, and a hider who has eaten, charged their phone and scoped the circle makes fewer forced errors.

These are the 24 strongest picks — gold beacons on the map above. Stops flagged corridor inferred aren't printed timepoints, so verify their routes on a live tracker before you commit.

Mall reality check

Malls look like ideal amenity zones — indoor, bathrooms, food courts — but almost none of them are actually inside a stop's hiding circle. (And remember: a business is a poor final spot; use malls to wait out the mid-round, not the end game.)

Hider Tactics

The playbook, distilled from the rulebook

Everything below follows from the official Hide+Seek rules — zone mechanics, end-game triggers, question fine print — applied to this specific map.

  1. Spend the whole 60 minutes. Your hiding window is your only free movement; anything you can't reach in one seat from Rapid Central is effectively out of play. Wherever you're standing when the timer ends is your zone — plan the ride, not just the destination.

  2. Scope your endgame spot early, then wander. While waiting in-zone you may shop, eat, or sightsee — but the instant seekers enter your zone off-transit, you lock in place. Know exactly where you'll be standing, and pre-take likely photo answers so a 10-minute photo timer never forces you to sprint.

  3. Parks are best-in-class final spots. Publicly accessible at all hours, no getting-kicked-out risk, and path networks satisfy the 10-feet-of-a-marked-path rule. Bonus: park questions measure to the map icon, so a big park lets you be in a park while answering "nearest park" with a different one.

  4. Businesses are mid-round tools, not hiding spots. The rulebook explicitly warns against stores as final spots (loitering suspicion, business hours vs. all-game-hours accessibility). Use Meijer and the malls for bathrooms, food and climate — then be somewhere public when the endgame starts.

  5. Respect the tentacle categories. Museums, libraries, movie theaters and hospitals within 1 mile of the seeker are the four MEDIUM tentacle questions — the mile is measured from them, not from you, so a target two miles from your zone can still be the name you have to say. A unique nearby library names your zone outright; a zone where the category is ambiguous or absent blunts the whole family — and a null answer still pays you a card draw.

  6. Radar targets you, not your zone. If the ring clips your circle but not your body, the answer is no. Stand on the far side of your ¼-mile disc from the seekers' likely approach — the simulator above shows exactly which zones turn amber.

  7. Build the deck to end holding time. Time bonuses only count if they're in your hand when the round ends — aim for roughly half bonuses, a quarter powerups, a quarter curses. Remember Move costs your entire hand, reveals your original station, and cannot be played in the end game.

  8. Watch the service clock. Last inbound trips run ~21:30–23:20 on weekdays and Saturdays, but Sundays collapse to hourly service ending ~18:00. Late in the day, an hourly-route zone has no Move escape — and the seekers know it. On Sundays, check the day banner above before you even shortlist a zone.
