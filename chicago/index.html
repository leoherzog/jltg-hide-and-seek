House rules to agree before you start

9 house rules fired for this map, in the order they matter. Each one has a predicate over the numbers above; rules whose precondition is false are not printed at all. The last one always fires, because the rulebook demands that conversation and explicitly refuses to automate it.

    why?
  2. This network has no dominant hub, so agree a start station before you begin. The three busiest are Michigan & Washington, Washington & Clark/Dearborn, 95th Street & Michigan, Michigan & Van Buren/Ida B. Wells.
    why?
  3. Use exactly these borders: south 41.640545, west -87.909076, north 42.076773, east -87.527928. The rulebook is emphatic that every player must be using the same set of borders, and on this map the border decides which questions work at all. Copy the GeoJSON rather than redrawing it.

  4. Read the dead list out before the first round. 14 questions here return null or a known answer — 1st Administrative Division, 2nd Administrative Division, Landmass, 1st Administrative Division Border, 2nd Administrative Division Border… — and asking one costs the seekers a slot and pays the hider a card.
    why?
  5. Decide as a group whether anyone has to spend money during the game. Egg Partner, Impressionable Consumer and Lemon Phylactery all require a purchase; the rulebook flags the first two and is silent about the third, which is an inconsistency. Treat them as one switch.
    why?
  6. Set an end-of-game timer at 23:23. That is 30 minutes before the median last departure, which is the point after which a hider in an average zone can no longer get anywhere — including home.
    why?
  7. A MEDIUM game gives 10 minutes to answer a photo question and 5 minutes for everything else, and the Move powerup grants 20 minutes. Put a visible timer on it.
    why?
  8. Hand limit is 6, raised to 7 or 8 only by Draw 1 Expand 1. Going over forces an immediate play-or-discard, and time bonuses only count if you are still holding them at the end.
    why?
  9. Before anything else, agree which areas are off the map because someone does not feel safe going there.   • Counts reflect one OpenStreetMap snapshot; the mirror that answered is not recorded, because recording it would make two identical runs produce different pages. Cached responses keep reruns byte-identical.
  • Cuisine tokens rejected as dishes or super-national regions rather than countries: african, american-chinese, arab, asian, bagel, bakery, balkan, bar, bar_and_grill, barbecue, belizean, bistro…. Adjective tokens only — promoting a dish to a country would change the count.
  • Distances are measured to a computed area centroid, not to a map app's label anchor. On this map's park polygons the two differ by up to 30 m at the 90th percentile, which is a real fraction of the 402 m zone radius.
  • Every OpenStreetMap count here is a lower bound on what the seekers' map app will show. OSM is strong on parks, schools, places of worship, hospitals and libraries and materially incomplete on retail, restaurants and chains.
  • OSM has no review count, so the rulebook's “5 or more Google Reviews” legitimacy test is approximated by requiring a `name` tag. Measured effect on this kind of feed: a 5–10% trim, in the right direction, but not the same function.
  • OpenStreetMap tags `natural=coastline` on ocean and sea shorelines only, so a great lake carries none. 284 shore segments were derived from Lake Michigan — water bodies larger than the game map, whose shore bounds the map the way a coast does. Distances are measured to those segments.
  • The rulebook's “within 10 ft of a routable path” test was not evaluated: this map has 423,221 walkable ways, and asking a shared Overpass mirror to buffer all of them is not a polite request. Every candidate spot below is therefore marked verify-on-the-ground.
  • These queries returned a floor rather than a total and are marked partial: building.
